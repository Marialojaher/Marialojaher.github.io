Reading Reflections

In Plato’s Cave – Susan Sontag

In “In Plato’s Cave,” Susan Sontag explains how photography changes the way we see and understand the world around us. She relates photography to Plato’s allegory of the cave, where people often mistake shadows for reality. Sontag argues that photographs can sometimes replace real experiences of the moment, making us feel that we understand something simply because we have seen an image of it. Reading this made me reflect on how often I take photos and how easily images can change my perception of the real world. For me, taking photos is a way to relax and capture moments that are very meaningful. I often photograph beautiful places, objects, or small details that give me a sense of calm or connection, such as city streets at night, museums, or moments with friends. Before reading Sontag, I considered photography primarily as something positive, a way to remember things for keepsakes or to express creativity. However, this reading helped me realize that photography can also distance us from real experiences. Sometimes, instead of fully living in the moment, we focus more on getting the perfect shot, or sometimes we even go to certain places just for the photos and not to enjoy the experience itself. Sontag explains that photographs give us a sense of control over the world. By turning moments into images, we freeze them and make them easier for the world to consume. This idea made me reflect on social media and how images are constantly shared, liked, commented on, and scrolled through. Images can make serious situations seem ordinary because we see them so frequently that we become desensitized. At the same time, photos can help raise awareness about things we wouldn't otherwise see. This contradiction was what struck me most, photography can both reveal and conceal the reality in which we live. As a multimedia content intern, I am beginning to understand that images are never neutral. Every photo involves decisions, such as framing, lighting, and timing. These decisions influence how others interpret the image. Throughout this semester, as I work on publishing posts and projects using graphics and text, I want to be more mindful of the posts I post on social media. Instead of simply creating images that look good, I want to think about the message they convey and what they omit so that students better understand what I post on social media. Visually representing our world today seems especially important to me because we live in an era where images travel faster than words. Through photography or illustration combined with text, I believe it's possible to show both beauty and complexity. For example, an image of a busy street could represent connection, but also isolation. Adding text could guide the viewer to reflect more deeply on what the image represents. Sontag's ideas remind me that images should foster reflection, not replace understanding. Overall, "In Plato's Cave" prompted me to think more critically about photography. It helped me see image making not just as documentation, but as a powerful tool that changes how people perceive the world. As I continue to develop my work this semester, I want each project to build upon the previous one, becoming more reflective, and aware of the influence of photography.
